About

I build machine learning models that keep working after deployment: on devices with little compute, and on data that drifts away from what the model was trained on.

My work so far asks how small and how adaptive a model can be before it stops being reliable. I have built an intrusion detector for medical IoT networks that updates in 0.65 seconds where retraining takes minutes, and a 2.03M-parameter crop-disease model that runs in 10.5 ms per image. I also co-developed a Wi-Fi sensing method that detects falls without wearables, published in IEEE Internet of Things Magazine.

I am an M.E. student in Computer Science at BITS Pilani, advised by Prof. Vinay Chamola, and I have held seven teaching assistantships there. Next, I want to carry the same discipline about efficiency and robustness into multimodal models and LLM agents.

A Novel Competing Convolutional Kernels Method to CSI-Based Fall Detection for Disabled People

Siva Sai, Taresh Bansal, Vinay Chamola

IEEE Internet of Things Magazine, 2025

  • Published
  • Q1 journal, impact factor 5.8

Detects falls from Wi-Fi channel state information, a non-wearable alternative for disabled and elderly people. Time series are classified with randomly generated convolutional kernels grouped into competing pattern dictionaries, using hard and soft counting and first-order differences. On a public dataset it reports 98% accuracy and 89% F1, about 9% above the prior state of the art.

Research

Drift-resilient intrusion detection for IoMT networks

Jan – Feb 2026

BITS Pilani. Basis of the paper under review at IEEE Internet of Things Magazine.

Question
Medical IoT traffic shifts over time and detectors go stale. Can a model keep up without being retrained from scratch?
Approach
Adaptive Hoeffding trees that reassess internal nodes as data arrives. Trained on the WUSTL-EHMS-2020 dataset, cleaned, with attack classes balanced by SMOTE.
Result
  • 99.94% accuracy, 9.05 points above the best incremental baseline, at 100% precision.
  • Updates in 0.65 s per cycle, against 41.5 s to retrain NuSVC and 199 s for Isolation Forest.

Lightweight architectures for plant disease recognition on edge devices

Mar – Aug 2026

BITS Pilani.

Question
Which small models are actually worth deploying on phones for crop-disease detection?
Approach
Compared 9 models under 2.2M parameters across 3 datasets (118k images, up to 115 diseases), including a proposed 2.03M-parameter model. Ablated CBAM attention and timed inference over 200 runs.
Result
  • The proposed model was best overall, with top accuracy on 2 of 3 datasets.
  • On the hardest, 115-class dataset it led the next model by 3.8 points (69.8% against 66.0%).
  • CBAM attention hurt rather than helped, costing 3.4 points on the hardest dataset.
  • 10.5 ms per image, 40% faster than the other Ghost-based models (17–18 ms).

Ask My Warehouse

Aug – Sep 2026

Ask business questions in plain English and get answers from a real data warehouse, with the SQL shown, role-based access, and human approval for every data change.

  • PySpark ETL over 541,909 retail transactions: duplicates removed, non-sales rows quarantined with a reason, and 16 automatic data-quality checks that stop the pipeline on failure.
  • PostgreSQL star schema (one fact table, four dimensions) served through an MCP server to a LangChain agent. Standard numbers come from 8 verified metrics built from tested SQL; anything else is labelled as custom SQL.
  • Security in three layers: Postgres roles with column-level grants, MCP tools chosen per role, and human approval for every admin change, written to an audit log in the same transaction. The AI cannot approve its own change.
  • Gemini first, with automatic fallback to a local Ollama model when a call fails.

PySpark, PostgreSQL, MCP, LangChain, LangGraph, FastAPI

Distributed movie-ticket booking on gRPC and Raft

2025

A booking service designed so that concurrent clients never oversell seats, and so the cluster keeps a leader when a node fails.

  • gRPC booking, admin and assistant services defined in Protocol Buffers, with per-show locks around seat reservation and a server-streaming seat-availability feed.
  • An admin RPC that launches concurrent simulated clients; integration tests assert that confirmed bookings never exceed available seats.
  • A three-node Raft cluster over gRPC: randomized election timeouts, RequestVote and AppendEntries RPCs, and leader heartbeats.
  • A local Hugging Face Transformers assistant that answers booking FAQs without an external API.

Python, gRPC, Protocol Buffers, Raft, FastAPI, Transformers, pytest
