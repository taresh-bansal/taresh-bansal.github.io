Taresh Bansal

AI Agents · Multimodal AI · AI for Science

M.E. Computer Science, BITS Pilani · Teaching Assistant, Introduction to LLMs

I work on AI agents and agentic AI, multimodal AI and AI for science: building models that reason, act and see reliably enough to be trusted with real decisions. I am an M.E. student in Computer Science at BITS Pilani (GPA 8.68/10), advised by Prof. Vinay Chamola.

Currently, I am working on polite masking in NLP, and on auditing visual grounding in medical vision-language models through controlled image interventions.

Earlier, in my first-author paper under review at IEEE Internet of Things Magazine, I built a drift-resilient intrusion detector for Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) networks. I co-authored a Wi-Fi fall-detection method published in the same magazine in 2025, and I benchmarked compact models for crop-disease recognition.

I have held seven teaching assistantships at BITS Pilani, currently for Introduction to LLMs, where students build an LLM from scratch. I am seeking PhD positions starting 2027 in agentic, multimodal and scientific AI.

Selected publications

  1. IoT Security

    Drift-Resilient Intrusion Detection in IoMT Networks Using Adaptive Hoeffding Trees with Internal Node Reassessment

    Taresh Bansal, Siva Sai, Vinay Chamola, Mohan Gurusamy

    IEEE Internet of Things Magazine, Under review

    An incremental intrusion detector for Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) network traffic. Adaptive Hoeffding trees with internal node reassessment re-check earlier splits as new traffic arrives, so the model adapts to distribution shift by updating on the fly instead of being retrained from scratch. On the WUSTL-EHMS-2020 dataset it is more accurate than both the incremental and the batch-retrained baselines, and each update is faster than retraining any of the batch models.

  2. Wireless Sensing

    A Novel Competing Convolutional Kernels Method to CSI-Based Fall Detection for Disabled People

    Siva Sai, Taresh Bansal, Vinay Chamola

    IEEE Internet of Things Magazine, 2025

    DOI

    Detects falls from Wi-Fi channel state information (CSI), a non-wearable alternative for disabled and elderly people. Time series are classified with randomly generated convolutional kernels grouped into competing pattern dictionaries, using hard and soft counting and first-order differences.
