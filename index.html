Taresh Bansal

M.E. Computer Science, BITS Pilani (GPA 8.68/10) · IEEE IoT Magazine author · Teaching Assistant, Introduction to LLMs

I study how to keep machine learning models reliable under tight compute budgets and shifting data, with a focus on IoT security and on-device deep learning. I am an M.E. student in Computer Science at BITS Pilani, advised by Prof. Vinay Chamola.

As first author, I built a drift-resilient intrusion detector for Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) networks using adaptive Hoeffding trees. It reaches 99.94% accuracy and updates in 0.65 s per cycle, against 41.5 s to retrain NuSVC; the paper is under review at IEEE Internet of Things Magazine. I co-authored a Wi-Fi fall-detection method published in the same magazine in 2025, and I benchmarked 9 compact models for crop-disease recognition, where the proposed 2.03M-parameter model ran at 10.5 ms per image (200-run average).

I have held seven teaching assistantships at BITS Pilani, most recently for Introduction to LLMs, where 50+ students build an LLM from scratch. Next, I want to make vision-language models small and robust enough to run on the kind of constrained devices I have worked with. I am seeking PhD positions starting 2027.
