Fall detection from Wi-Fi signals

2025 · IEEE Internet of Things Magazine

Wireless sensing · Health monitoring

Spotting falls from ordinary Wi-Fi, with nothing to wear

Wearable fall sensors are uncomfortable, need charging and are often left off. Wi-Fi signals change as a person moves through a room, and this method reads those changes to tell a fall from everyday movement.

Aim

Detect falls of disabled and elderly people from ordinary Wi-Fi signals, with nothing to wear and accurately enough to trust.

What was done

  1. Sense with Wi-Fi. As a person moves, they change how Wi-Fi signals travel between a router and a receiver. A public dataset records these changes while people walk, sit, stand, lie down, run and fall.
  2. Look for patterns that compete. Many small random patterns slide along the signal. They are split into groups, and inside each group only the closest match is counted at each moment.
  3. Classify the counts. The counts, taken from the signal and from how fast it changes, go to a simple classifier that answers one question: fall or not?
  4. Compare. Against three deep-learning models and the best previously published method on the same data.

What was found

Reading the results

Falls are rare, so a model can look accurate while missing them. The fair test is two plain questions: how many real falls were caught, and how many alarms were real falls?

  • Catches more real falls than any model it was compared with.
  • Raises fewer false alarms than any model it was compared with.
  • Clearly ahead of the best published method on the measure that balances both.

Why it matters

Wearable sensors are uncomfortable, need charging and are often left off. Wi-Fi is already in most homes and care settings, so fall detection can run in the background, with nothing for the person to remember.
