CSI-based fall detection

2025

Wireless sensing · Time-series classification

Detecting falls from Wi-Fi signals, without wearables

Wi-Fi channel state information (CSI) changes as people move. This paper classifies those changes to detect falls of disabled and elderly people without any wearable device.

Read the paper (DOI)BibTeX

Problem

Wearable fall detectors only work when they are worn and charged. A detector that reads the Wi-Fi signals already in the room has no such dependence.

Approach

CSI streams are treated as time series and classified with randomly generated convolutional kernels, grouped into pattern dictionaries that compete with each other (“competing kernels”). Features come from hard and soft counting of kernel matches and from first-order differences of the signal.

Results

On a public CSI dataset the method improves on the best previously reported F1 score.

Publication

Siva Sai, Taresh Bansal, Vinay Chamola. “A Novel Competing Convolutional Kernels Method to CSI-Based Fall Detection for Disabled People.” IEEE Internet of Things Magazine, 2025. doi.org/10.1109/MIOT.2025.3588213
