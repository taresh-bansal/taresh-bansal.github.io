LightGhost: plant disease on tiny devices

Mar – Aug 2026 · Sole author

Efficient models · Agriculture

A tiny model that recognises crop disease in real field photos

Farm sensors and cheap phones have little memory and no special chip, so accurate models do not fit and small ones struggle on messy field photos. This work trains a tiny model with help from a larger teacher, and tests where that helps and where it does not.

Aim

Build a plant-disease model small enough for cheap phones and farm sensors that still works on real field photos, and test honestly whether smaller also means faster.

What was done

  1. Teach a small model with a large one. A large, accurate model (the teacher) shows a tiny one (the student) not just the right answer but which diseases look alike. This is called knowledge distillation.
  2. Train on harder images. Photos are blended and altered during training, and teacher and student always see the same version.
  3. Test on three kinds of data. A large clean multi-crop set, real photos taken in the field, and a set with many look-alike diseases.
  4. Compare broadly. Against standard small models and several modified designs, on accuracy, size, how trustworthy the confidence is, and speed.

What was found

  • Best accuracy for its size among the models accurate enough to use.
  • Biggest gains on real field photos, where the standard small model struggles most.
  • A reliable shortlist. For most field photos the right disease is in its top three, and its confidence scores are well calibrated.
  • Small is not the same as fast. Despite being one of the smallest models, it was slower than its much larger teacher, because its building blocks wait on memory rather than arithmetic.

Why it matters

A farmer's phone or a field sensor needs a model that fits and still works outdoors. This work shows a teacher can make a tiny model far more useful on field photos, and warns that model size alone does not predict speed on a real device.

Each model was trained once, and speed was measured on a laptop rather than on farm hardware; both are noted as limits in the paper.
