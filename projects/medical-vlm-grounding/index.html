Auditing visual grounding in medical VLMs

2026 · In progress

Multimodal AI · Medical imaging

Do medical AI models actually look at the X-ray?

Models that write chest X-ray reports are graded on how closely their report matches the radiologist's. That grade cannot tell whether the model looked at the image: a convincing report can be written from what patients usually look like. This study tests image use directly, by changing the image and watching what happens to the report.

Code on GitHubResults summary

Problem

Report-agreement scores reward a fluent, typical-looking report whether or not it came from the image. A model can score well while largely ignoring the X-ray, and standard evaluation has no way to tell.

Approach

Controlled interventions on the image, with the prompt, decoding and patient held fixed. Each model writes a report under five conditions:

The five image conditions and what each one tests
ConditionWhat the model is givenWhat it tests
RealThe correct X-rayNormal behaviour
SwappedAnother patient's X-rayDoes the report follow the image it was given?
BlankA black imageBehaviour with an uninformative image
Metadata onlyNo image; acquisition details as textInfluence of non-image cues
No inputNothingThe model's built-in “default patient”

The key measure is source-following: the swapped-image report is scored against both the swapped-in patient's report and the original patient's. A model that reads the image follows the new X-ray; one that ignores it does not. Free-text reports have no single answer to flip, so the method adds two-reference scoring, finding-level analysis and chest X-ray specialists as positive controls. Hypotheses were preregistered, and every scorer was first validated against radiologists' judgements.

Findings

  • A clear, consistent gradient. Models range from strongly image-reading to essentially guessing, in the same order on every dataset and scorer: CheXagent, MAIRA-2, MedGemma, Lingshu, Qwen2.5-VL.
  • Specialists read the image more than medical generalists. The preregistered primary hypothesis, confirmed on all three scorers.
  • Medical generalists carry a “sick patient” prior. With no image, Lingshu and MedGemma describe a typical sick inpatient, while the specialists and the general-purpose model default to normal or empty output. That prior earns credit without looking.
  • Standard grading cannot see the difference. Lingshu scores about as well as MAIRA-2 on conventional agreement, yet earns a large share of its score with no image at all.
  • No model notices when it cannot see. Every model describes a black image as a normal chest X-ray.

Why it matters

Recent work audited image use in yes/no medical question answering and named free-text report generation as the open next step. This study takes that step, and shows that a model's headline score and its reliance on the image can come apart, which matters wherever these models are trusted to read scans.

Study design

Models
Chest X-ray specialists (CheXagent, MAIRA-2); medical generalists (MedGemma, Lingshu); general-purpose base model (Qwen2.5-VL)
Data
MIMIC-CXR, IU X-ray and CheXpert Plus; scorer validation on ReXVal
Scorers
RadGraph F1 (primary), an LLM judge, and CheXbert, each validated against radiologists
Design
Two preregistered studies; deterministic decoding; everything except the image held fixed
Status
Both studies complete; robustness checks and paper in progress
