Do medical AI models actually look at the X-ray?

2026 · Medical AI · In progress

Multimodal AI · Medical imaging

A good grade does not prove the model looked

AI models that write chest X-ray reports are graded on how closely their report matches the radiologist's. A convincing report can be written from what patients usually look like, without reading the image at all. This study tests that directly.

Aim

Find out whether medical AI models write their reports from the X-ray in front of them, or from habit.

What was done

  1. Change only the image. Same patient, same instructions. The model gets the right X-ray, another patient's X-ray, a black image, only the scan details as text, or nothing.
  2. Let five models write a report each time. Two chest X-ray specialists, two general medical models, and one general-purpose model.
  3. Grade each report twice. Against the right patient's report and against the swapped-in patient's, using three graders checked against radiologists.

What was found

1. Some models read the image. Others barely look.

2. Some models earn most of their grade without the image.

3. The usual grade cannot tell them apart.

4. With nothing to see, models invent a patient.

Why it matters

A high score can hide a model that is guessing. Before these models are trusted to read scans, they should be tested on whether they look, not only on whether they sound right. This study gives a simple way to do that, and was planned and registered before any analysis.

Study at a glance

Models
CheXagent and MAIRA-2 (chest X-ray specialists); MedGemma and Lingshu (general medical models); Qwen2.5-VL (general-purpose)
Data
Three public chest X-ray collections: MIMIC-CXR, IU X-ray and CheXpert Plus
Grading
Three independent graders, each first checked against radiologists' judgements
Status
Both studies complete; robustness checks and paper in progress
